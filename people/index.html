Xiaoying Yang (Current Student)

PhD candidate, Environmental Planning and Design at Myers-Lawson School of Construction, Virginia Tech

My research focuses on intelligent and human-centered construction systems that integrate wearable sensing, immersive technologies, artificial intelligence, and human–robot collaboration. I study how workers’ physiological and cognitive states can be monitored and understood during construction activities. Statistical and machine learning methods are used to analyze multimodal data and support real-time assessment. The goal of my research is to improve construction safety, performance, worker well-being, and interaction between workers and emerging technologies.

I’m a planter, birder, fishing guy! See the world, live with questions.

Hanwen Ju (Current Student)

PhD student, Environmental Planning and Design at Myers-Lawson School of Construction, Virginia Tech

I am a Ph.D. student at Virginia Tech, specializing in smart construction, human–robot collaboration, and construction automation. My research focuses on integrating emerging technologies, including augmented reality (AR), robotics, and artificial intelligence (AI), to enhance construction efficiency, safety, and productivity.

My current work explores AR-assisted human–robot collaboration, with a particular emphasis on human cognitive performance, decision-making, and robotic interaction in construction environments. Through interdisciplinary research, I aim to develop intelligent, human-centered construction systems that bridge the gap between technological innovation and real-world applications.

I am passionate about advancing digital transformation and automation in the construction industry through innovative research and practical solutions.

Yingjie Zhao (Current Student)

PhD student, Environmental Planning and Design at Myers-Lawson School of Construction, Virginia Tech

My research explores AI-powered robotics and intelligent automation for construction. I develop robotic systems that combine computer vision, autonomous navigation, motion planning, and adaptive control to perform material transportation and assembly tasks in complex and dynamic environments.

By connecting robotic perception, decision-making, and physical operation, I aim to create practical construction technologies that enhance productivity, accuracy, and safety. My broader goal is to help build a future in which humans and intelligent machines work together more effectively on construction sites.

Outside of research, I enjoy staying active, playing sports, and traveling to experience new places, cultures, and perspectives. Always moving, always exploring, and always curious about what comes next.
